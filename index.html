About this tracker

What is a ransomware leak site?

Most ransomware gangs run double-extortion operations: they steal data before encrypting systems, then publish the victim's name on a data leak site (DLS), usually hosted on Tor, to pressure it into paying. If no ransom is paid, the stolen files are often released there.

Where does the data come from?

Posts are collected from ransomware.live, which monitors hundreds of leak sites. This tracker fetches its API three times a day (06:33, 12:33 and 18:33 UTC), adds country and group information, and publishes the result as this table, pages per group, country and sector, a JSON dataset and an Atom feed.

Are the listings verified?

No. A post is a claim made by a criminal group. Some gangs exaggerate, re-post old breaches or list organisations they never compromised. Treat each entry as an indicator that needs confirmation, not as proof of a breach.

My organisation is listed — what now?

Contact your incident response provider and your national CERT or CSIRT, and report the incident to law enforcement. If personal data may be affected, check your breach-notification duties, for example under the GDPR. Before paying anything, look for free decryptors on No More Ransom.

Can I reuse the data?

Yes. Download the full JSON dataset or the last 30 days, subscribe to the Atom feed, or read the source code on GitHub. Please credit ransomware.live as the original source.
